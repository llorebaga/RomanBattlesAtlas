01

EVENT OVERVIEW

The fall of Capua showed that defection to Hannibal could not be protected, and the war’s momentum began to shift to Rome.

Rome besieged Capua, which had defected after Cannae; Hannibal’s march toward Rome failed to draw off the besiegers, and the city fell in 211.

Capua was the second city of Italy and its defection after Cannae was the worst political loss of the war. Recovering it was the test of whether Hannibal's Italian strategy could work at all: he had told the communities of the south that Rome could not protect its own and could not punish theirs. Rome answered not with a battle but with engineering — a double line of ditch and rampart, one facing the city and one facing outward — and then simply refused to be moved, even when Hannibal marched on Rome itself to make it move.

EVIDENCE REGISTER

What remains uncertain

  • The chronology of the siege and Hannibal’s demonstration against Rome is compressed in the sources.
