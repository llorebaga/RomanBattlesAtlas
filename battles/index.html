The wars with Veii 449–391 BCE

◎Siege

Siege of Veii

406–396 BCE (traditional)

Rome besieged its nearest rival, an Etruscan city on a plateau twelve miles away, and after a siege the tradition made ten years long took it, sold its people, and annexed its land.

Result Roman capture and destruction of the city

Veii, on the Cremera plateau twelve miles north of Rome

The Gallic catastrophe and recovery 390–350 BCE

⚔Land battle

Battle of the Allia

390 BCE (Roman reckoning; 387/6 in the Greek)

A Gallic war band destroyed a Roman army eleven miles from the city and then took Rome itself, holding everything but the Capitol until it was bought off.

Result Catastrophic Roman defeat; Rome sacked

The Allia stream, about eleven miles north of Rome

The Latin and first Samnite wars 349–327 BCE

⚔Land battle

Battle of Vesuvius

340 BCE

Rome defeated its own Latin and Campanian allies, who had demanded equality in the alliance and gone to war when refused. The consul Decius Mus was said to have devoted himself and the enemy to the gods of the underworld and ridden alone into the Latin line.

Result Roman victory

Near Veseris, at the foot of Vesuvius (site unidentified)

The Pyrrhic War 290–272 BCE

⚔Land battle

Battle of Heraclea

280 BCE

Rome's first battle against a Hellenistic army. Pyrrhus held the Romans at the river crossing with his cavalry, ground them with the pike phalanx, and broke them with elephants they had never seen before.

Result Epirote victory

Near Heraclea on the Siris, Lucania (modern Policoro)

Full account View on map
⚔Land battle

Battle of Asculum

279 BCE

Pyrrhus beat Rome a second time, over two days and on ground the Romans had chosen to blunt his phalanx. The losses among his own veterans and officers gave the language a phrase for a victory that ruins the winner.

Result Epirote victory, at a cost he could not afford

Near Asculum in Apulia (modern Ascoli Satriano)

⚔Land battle

Battle of Beneventum

275 BCE

Back from Sicily and weaker than when he left, Pyrrhus attempted a night march to surprise a Roman camp, arrived in daylight and disordered, and was fought to a standstill. He returned to Epirus and the war ended.

Result Roman success; Pyrrhus withdraws from Italy

Near Malventum, Samnium — renamed Beneventum afterwards
