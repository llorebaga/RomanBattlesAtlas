01

EVENT OVERVIEW

The first battle of the war that ended the Latin League. Because the two sides were armed and drilled alike, it was also the point at which Roman writers began to describe war as decided by discipline rather than by advantage.

Rome defeated its own Latin and Campanian allies, who had demanded equality in the alliance and gone to war when refused. The consul Decius Mus was said to have devoted himself and the enemy to the gods of the underworld and ridden alone into the Latin line.

The Latin cities had fought beside Rome for a century and a half and wanted the standing that went with it: shared magistracies, a real say in where the armies went. Rome refused, and the alliance broke. The awkwardness of the war — Romans against Latins who fought in the same formations with the same weapons — shapes everything the sources say about it, including the two famous stories attached to this battle: Manlius executing his own son for leaving the ranks to fight, and Decius' devotio.

HOW THE BATTLE DEVELOPED

Stage by stage

Veseris is unlocated and Livy's account of the fighting is built on the manipular legion of his own day, which did not yet exist. What survives is the devotio of Decius — a religious act, not a manoeuvre.

EVIDENCE REGISTER

What remains uncertain

  • Veseris is unlocated.
  • The devotio of Decius, repeated by his son at Sentinum and possibly his grandson, looks like a family tradition shaped over generations.
  • Livy's tactical detail for this battle is his own reconstruction.

This page separates surviving testimony from modern reconstruction. It does not invent precise tracks, dialogue, weather, or unit positions.
